# Contact encoder–impulse decoder study: results (milestone, 2026-08-28)

Question: does a physics-grounded contact bottleneck — geometry encoder → per-contact impulse decoder → deterministic
6-D wrench → transition model — improve the closed-loop box-drop rollouts over the existing 64-D pooled contact context
(BASE-64)?  Everything else is matched: frozen 30 k point encoder, `pilot1b` splits, learned contacts recomputed from the
predicted state, gravity + gyroscopic priors, hard contact gate, transformer size, steps, batch, event oversampling.
Full log with the diagnosis trail: `docs/PROGRESS.md` §9.  Plan and review: this directory.

## What made the decoder work (each was necessary; `docs/PROGRESS.md` §9.5–9.7)

1. Exact Chrono gap from the predicted contact point: `d = h − (Σ_a|R[2,a]| − 1)·envelope` (median residual 0.0000 mm);
   the NSC first-impact impulse follows gap stabilization `v'_n = −d/dt`, then restitution — sub-mm sensitivity that the
   regressed `d` (0.5 mm error) cannot provide.
2. Yaw augmentation (exact symmetry) — removed memorization of the ~1,200 training impacts; fixed the angular channel.
3. Closed-form frictional single-contact impulse (stick / Coulomb slip, Chrono's normal-velocity regimes) as prior
   features and head scale — the friction–normal coupling was the last 2× in the normal impulse.
4. No slot embedding (explicit geometry + dynamics features suffice), physically scaled head, near-L1 loss.
5. The exact gyroscopic free-flight prior (also given to BASE-64: `Δω = −dt·R·I_b⁻¹(ω_b × I_b ω_b)`).
